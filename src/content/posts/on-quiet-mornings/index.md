There is an hour before the rest of the world logs in. The kettle ticks. The radiator knocks twice, like it does every morning, as if to check that someone is listening.

I used to fill that hour with email. I would open the laptop before I had opened the curtains, and by the time the coffee was ready I had already read three things that made me anxious and two that made me feel behind. The day began as a reply.

Now I fill it with nothing in particular. I read a few pages of whatever book is on the arm of the chair. I write a paragraph that nobody will see. I watch the light come up over the building across the street, the way it hits the fourth floor first and then slides down, window by window.

Somehow that turns out to be the most productive thing I do all day. Not because the hour produces anything, but because it is the only part of the day that is not a response to something else.

## The rules, such as they are

There are only two. No screens until the coffee is finished, and no deciding anything important. Deciding can wait until nine. The hour is for noticing.

I break both rules about once a week. That's fine. The point was never discipline. The point was to remember that the day can start with a question of my own instead of an answer to someone else's.
